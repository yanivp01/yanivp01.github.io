Long ago, mankind spoke one language. In our greatness, we built a tower to the heavens. To stop us from this hubris, our languages were divided, spawning corporate jargon, leaving us forever unable to unite again.

Today, I decode 3 terms in Venture Capital, so you can understand the secret language of venture capitalists.

## 1\. Investor Conviction

Not all investments are made by consensus — many are driven by high conviction. A VC with conviction believes strongly in a startup's potential, even when others hesitate.

*   **Consensus Investing:** A startup gets backed when most partners at a VC firm agree. This often leads to "safe bets."
*   **Conviction Investing:** A partner takes a strong stance, even if others are skeptical, leading to bold, high-reward investments.
*   **High vs. Diversified Conviction Portfolios:** Some VCs double down on a few startups (high conviction), while others spread risk across many (diversified).

## 2\. Post-Investment: What Happens After the Money Hits?

Funding is just the beginning — what comes next is equally critical.

*   **Board Involvement:** VCs often take a seat at the table, helping shape strategic decisions.
*   **Portfolio Support:** From hiring and partnerships to follow-on funding, the best VCs act as long-term allies.
*   **Founder–VC Dynamics:** Navigating this relationship is crucial — some VCs are hands-off, while others expect active engagement.

## 3\. Secondary Sale: Liquidity Before an Exit

A secondary sale lets early investors, employees, or even founders sell shares before an IPO or acquisition.

*   **Primary vs. Secondary:** In a primary round, the company sells new shares. In a secondary sale, existing shares are sold between investors.
*   **Why It Matters:** Early-stage investors often cash out in later rounds, allowing fresh capital to enter. Founders can also de-risk without waiting for a full exit.
*   **Common Buyers:** Later-stage VCs, private equity, or even strategic investors.
